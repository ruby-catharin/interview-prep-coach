# Profile Intake (Mode 1)

Ask in batches of 3–4. Skip anything already answered by the resume or an existing profile. Voice answers are welcome — extract and structure them yourself.

## Batch A — Direction
1. What's your goal for this search? (next role, step up in level, switch domain, relocate, comp jump…)
2. What are your dream roles and companies? Why those?
3. What does "the right job" look like in 2–3 years if this goes well?

## Batch B — What you're looking for
4. Team and culture you thrive in — and ones you've struggled in?
5. Problems/products you want to work on?
6. Level/title you're targeting, and how confident you are you're ready for it?

## Batch C — Non-negotiables & constraints
7. Non-negotiables for your next role (e.g. end-to-end ownership, direct customer access, breadth of scope, a specific domain).
8. Location, remote/hybrid, relocation, work authorisation.
9. Compensation floor and target (optional, private, used only for screening practice and fit checks).
10. Timeline: when do you need or want to move? Notice period?
11. Hard no's (industries, company stages, travel, on-call…)?
12. Longer-term plans a role must fit (a move in 2 years, a side project, studies…)?

## Batch D — Strengths and worries
11. Three things you're genuinely better at than most peers — with proof.
12. What question are you most afraid of being asked?
13. Gaps an interviewer might probe (short tenure, career switch, missing skill, layoff…)?

## Batch E — Story inventory (the most important part)
For each of 6–10 stories, capture:
- **Title** (short handle, e.g. "Voice agent latency trade-off")
- **Situation** — context, stakes, constraint (1–2 lines)
- **Your action** — what *you* did, decisions and trade-offs, not "we"
- **Result** — numbers: revenue, %, time, users, retention, CSAT, latency, cost
- **Proves** — competencies (ownership, influence without authority, conflict, failure/learning, ambiguity, prioritisation, technical depth, customer insight, leadership, speed)
- **Best for** — which question types it answers

Prompts to surface stories:
- Proudest product/project shipped end-to-end
- A time you were wrong / failed and what changed after
- A conflict with engineering, design, a stakeholder, or your manager
- A decision with incomplete data
- Influencing someone senior without authority
- Saying no / killing a feature
- A time you went deep technically
- Turning around something that was going badly

Push for numbers. If the user doesn't know one, write `[confirm number]` and move on.
